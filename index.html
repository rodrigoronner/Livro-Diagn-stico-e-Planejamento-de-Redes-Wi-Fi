Channel Planning
2,4 GHz 2412–2472 MHz · 13 channels · 22 MHz width
Non-overlapping channel (1, 6, 11)
Overlapping channel — avoid
Mandatory rule: use only channels 1, 6, and 11 in 2.4 GHz. Any other channel causes Adjacent Channel Interference (ACI), which CSMA/CA does not detect and which is worse than co-channel interference. Always use 20 MHz width in this band. Ch. 9 Section 9.3.
5 GHz Over 20 non-overlapping channels · UNII-1 to UNII-3
UNII-1 (no DFS)
UNII-2A/2C (DFS required)
UNII-3 (no DFS)
DFS: the AP automatically releases the channel upon detecting a radar signal. In environments near airports, coastlines, or military bases, prefer UNII-1 (36–48) or UNII-3 (149–165). In urban environments without these sources, DFS channels are generally safe and offer extra spectrum.
Recommended width: residential → 80 MHz · dense corporate → 40 MHz · high density → 20 MHz. Ch. 9 Section 9.3 / Ch. 10 Section 10.2.
6 GHz Wi-Fi 6E / Wi-Fi 7 · Approved by Anatel 2021 (Resolution 754)

Exploring Modulations

An interactive guide to three techniques that put more data on the air: QAM modulation, which sets how many bits each symbol carries; OFDMA, which splits the channel in frequency; and MU-MIMO, which splits the channel in space. Each step has concepts, a simulator with a live reading and step-by-step experiments.

Step 1 · The basics

Before you start: three ideas

The carrier

A radio wave of fixed frequency, such as 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz. On its own it carries no information: it is only the vehicle. See its wavelength in the λ calculator.

Modulation

The way the carrier is changed to represent bits. Wi-Fi changes amplitude (wave height) and phase (where the wave starts) at the same time. Each combination is a symbol.

The channel is shared

All devices use the same channel. The time each one occupies it is called airtime. The less airtime each client spends, the more clients the network serves.

Analogy

Think of the channel as a road and data as parcels. QAM decides how many boxes fit in each truck. OFDMA lets one truck carry parcels for several clients in a single trip. MU-MIMO puts several trucks on different lanes at the same time, each going to one client.

Step 2 · Modulation

QAM constellation: how many bits fit in a symbol

How to read the chart

Each white dot is a possible symbol. Distance from the centre is the amplitude; the angle is the phase. Axes I and Q are two components of the same wave.

More points, more bits

With N points, each symbol carries log₂(N) bits.

16 → 4 bits · 1024 → 10 bits

The problem: noise

The signal arrives with noise and spreads around the right point (orange cloud). If it lands in a neighbour's region, the receiver decodes it wrong (red dot).

SNR

Signal-to-noise ratio, in dB. The more points in the constellation, the higher the SNR needed. Thresholds come from the SNR × MCS table in the References tab.

Step 3 · Sharing the channel

OFDMA × MU-MIMO: frequency, time and space

Frequency axis

A 20 MHz channel has hundreds of subcarriers. Wi-Fi 6 groups them into up to 9 RUs (resource units of 26 subcarriers).

Time axis

Each column is a slot: one transmission. Fewer slots to serve everyone means less airtime spent.

Space axis

With several antennas the AP creates spatial streams: beams that reach different clients at the same time, on the same frequency.

How to read the cube

Each small cube is 1 RU, in 1 slot, on 1 stream. Colour marks the client. A transparent cube is padding: it occupies the channel without carrying data.

Step 4 · Space

MU-MIMO beams in the room

Beamforming

By adjusting the signal on each antenna, the AP concentrates energy in one direction, forming a beam for each client.

Angular separation

Two clients in the same direction get overlapping beams. The AP serves one now and the other in the next slot.

More antennas

More antennas form narrower beams and serve more clients at once: a 4×4 AP serves up to 4 single-antenna clients.

Glossary
Carrier
Fixed-frequency radio wave that is altered to carry data.
Symbol
A combination of amplitude and phase; it represents a group of bits.
QAM
Quadrature amplitude modulation: changes amplitude and phase together.
I and Q
In-phase and quadrature components of the wave; the axes of the constellation.
SNR
Signal-to-noise ratio, in dB. Sets which modulation the link supports.
MCS
Modulation and coding scheme; the device changes MCS with signal quality.
Subcarrier
Each of the small carriers into which OFDM splits the channel.
RU
Resource unit: group of subcarriers given to one client in OFDMA.
Airtime
Time a device occupies the channel.
Spatial stream
Independent data sequence sent over a different spatial path.
Beamforming
Technique that focuses the antennas' signal toward the client.
Sounding (NDP)
Test frame the AP sends to measure the channel before forming beams.
